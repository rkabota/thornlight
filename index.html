The idea

Thornlight grew out of Dark Bloom, a gothic botanical clothing label: roses, thorns and candlelight in a deep red palette. Most players treat the timeline as a plain line. Here it is a living stem, with thorns that sharpen as it fills, and a rose for a playhead that opens wider while you scrub. The controls live in a black-glass reliquary with a single crimson hairline, so they read as clearly over a sunny meadow as over a night scene.

Made to be used

  • Readable over anything: a solid glass plate, not just a gradient, keeps icons and digits at strong contrast on bright footage.
  • Keyboard first: every control is reachable with Tab, with a bone-and-crimson focus ring. Space or K plays, arrows seek and set volume, J and L skip 10 seconds, M mutes, F goes fullscreen, 0 to 9 jump through the film.
  • Thumb-sized: controls are 44 pixels or more, and on a phone the bar drops secondary buttons and keeps play, the timeline, time, volume and fullscreen.
  • Calm on request: with reduced motion on, the rose stops turning, the glow stops pulsing and fades become instant.
